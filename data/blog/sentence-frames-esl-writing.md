Sentence frames are the most underused scaffold in ESL writing. Done right, they reduce blank-page anxiety, accelerate vocabulary application, and bridge home language to English production. This guide curates 50+ ESL sentence frames organized by purpose and WIDA level.

For our complete library, see [ESL writing worksheets](/esl-writing-worksheets). For full prompts, read [ESL writing prompts by level](/teaching-tips/esl-writing-prompts-by-level).

## What Sentence Frames Are (and Aren't)

### What They Are
Pre-built sentence structures with strategic blanks. Students fill in vocabulary while the structure stays stable. Example: "I see a ___."

### What They Aren't
- Not the final goal (we want students writing original sentences eventually)
- Not crutches forever (Level 5 students need fewer frames)
- Not limiting (research shows frames enable, not constrain, ELL writing)

## How to Implement Sentence Frames

### Step 1: Display Visibly
Print frames on cardstock. Display on word wall, anchor chart, or laminated bookmark.

### Step 2: Model First
Always model the frame yourself before asking students to use it. Say it 3 times with different words.

### Step 3: Choral Practice
Whole class chants the frame with different words. Builds oral familiarity before written use.

### Step 4: Guided Practice
Small group or pairs use the frame with teacher support. Catch and correct gently.

### Step 5: Independent Use
Students use frames during writing. Always have frames visible during writing tasks.

### Step 6: Gradual Release
As students reach Level 4-5, fade frames. Encourage original sentence construction.

## Common Mistakes with Sentence Frames

### Mistake 1: Too Many at Once
Don't introduce 10 new frames at the same time. Pick 1-2 per week. Mastery before expansion.

### Mistake 2: Using Frames Forever
Level 5 students who still need frames are over-scaffolded. Gradual release matters.

### Mistake 3: Frame Without Vocabulary
Frame "I see a ___" needs vocabulary cards. Otherwise students fill in nothing.

### Mistake 4: No Bilingual Option for Level 1-2
Spanish-speaking newcomers benefit from bilingual frames. Don't skip this scaffold.
