If you teach English Learners, you've heard the terms "dual language classroom" and "bilingual classroom" used interchangeably. They sound the same. They are not. The difference matters because it shapes everything: your daily schedule, your materials, your assessment plan, and your parent communication.

This guide breaks down the four key differences and shows you how to choose resources that actually fit your model. For a full overview of dual language program models, see our [dual language classroom resources](/dual-language-classroom) page.

## Difference 1: Goal — Biliteracy vs English Proficiency

This is the most fundamental difference.

A **dual language classroom** has the explicit goal of producing fully biliterate students — students who can read, write, listen, speak, and think academically in two languages by Grade 5 or 8. Both languages are treated as long-term assets, not temporary scaffolds.

A **transitional bilingual classroom** uses the home language as a bridge to English proficiency. The goal is English-only academic instruction by Grade 2-3. The home language is phased out, not maintained.

**Why it matters for you:** If you're in a dual language classroom, you need parallel materials in both languages — bilingual vocabulary cards, sentence frames in both languages, and balanced parent communication. If you're in a transitional bilingual classroom, you need materials that gradually shift the language load toward English.

## Difference 2: Time Allocation

Dual language programs allocate substantial time to both languages — typically 30-50% in the target language by Grade 5, even in 90/10 models that start with more.

Transitional bilingual programs allocate decreasing time to the home language each year: maybe 70% in Kindergarten, 50% in Grade 1, 20% in Grade 2, and English-only by Grade 3.

ESL/ELD programs allocate **zero minutes** to the home language for instruction. The home language is welcomed but not used as a medium of teaching.

## Difference 3: Student Population

Dual language classrooms come in several flavors based on who's in the room:

- **One-way DL:** All students share one home language (often Spanish). Common in districts with a strong Spanish-speaking community.
- **Two-way immersion:** Roughly half native English speakers, half native Spanish speakers. Peers become language models for each other.
- **Maintenance bilingual:** Designed for heritage speakers who already speak the target language at home, with the goal of developing literacy in that language.

Transitional bilingual classrooms typically only enroll English Learners — students who don't yet speak English at grade level.

ESL/ELD pull-out groups include students from many language backgrounds together, since English is the common goal.

## Difference 4: Materials Required

This is where the practical impact hits your prep time.

A **dual language classroom** needs:

- Bilingual labels for every visible object (English-Spanish)
- Two parallel word walls
- Bilingual vocabulary cards and picture dictionaries
- Sentence frames in both languages
- Texts at grade level in both languages
- Bilingual parent communication (every note, every form)
- Visual schedules in both languages

A **transitional bilingual classroom** needs:

- Year 1: Most materials in home language, English support materials
- Year 2: Balanced materials with English emphasis
- Year 3: English-dominant materials with home-language scaffolds

An **ESL/ELD pull-out** needs:

- English-leveled texts (WIDA-aligned)
- Visual supports for newcomers
- Sentence frames in English
- Multilingual parent communication when possible

## Quick Comparison Table

| Feature | Dual Language | Transitional Bilingual | ESL / ELD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Biliteracy | English proficiency | English proficiency |
| **Home language used for instruction** | Long-term | 1-3 years | Never |
| **Student population** | Mixed or single-language | English Learners only | Mixed L1 backgrounds |
| **Typical duration** | K-12 | K-2 or K-3 | As needed |
| **Teacher language requirement** | Both languages | Both (early years) | English only |
| **Materials** | Parallel bilingual | Shifting bilingual | English-dominant |

## So Which One Are You In?

Ask yourself these three questions:

1. **Will my students still be learning content in their home language by Grade 5?** Yes → dual language. No → transitional or ESL.
2. **Is my classroom intentionally mixed with native speakers of both languages?** Yes → two-way immersion DL. No → one-way DL or transitional.
3. **Do I use the home language for academic content (not just translation)?** Yes → DL or transitional bilingual. No → ESL.
