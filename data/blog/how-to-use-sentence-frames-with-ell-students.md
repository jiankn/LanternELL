## What Are Sentence Frames?

Sentence frames are partially completed sentences that give English Language Learners a **structure to express their ideas**. Instead of facing a blank page or an open-ended question, students fill in key words while the grammar and syntax are already provided.

For example, instead of asking "What do you think about this story?", a sentence frame provides:

**"I think _____ because _____."**

This removes the cognitive overload of constructing a sentence from scratch and lets students focus on *meaning* rather than *mechanics*.

## Why Sentence Frames Work for ELLs

Research consistently shows that sentence frames are effective because they:

- **Lower the affective filter** — students feel safer participating when they have a structure to follow
- **Build academic language** — frames introduce formal vocabulary and complex syntax naturally
- **Support all proficiency levels** — newcomers can use simple frames while advanced learners tackle complex ones
- **Enable immediate participation** — even students with minimal English can contribute to class discussions on day one

## Grade-Level Strategies

### K-2: Visual + Oral Sentence Frames

Young ELLs benefit from sentence frames paired with **visual cues**. Display frames on the wall with picture supports and practice them orally before asking students to write.

**Example frames for K-2:**
- "I see a _____."
- "The _____ is _____." (color/size)
- "I like _____ because _____."

**Tip:** Use hand gestures or picture cards to represent the blank spaces. Students point to the picture, then say the word.

### 3-5: Academic Discussion Frames

By grades 3-5, sentence frames should push students toward **academic discourse**. Use frames that require students to *agree, disagree, compare, and elaborate*.

**Example frames for 3-5:**
- "I agree with _____ because _____."
- "My evidence shows that _____."
- "_____ is similar to _____ because _____."

**Tip:** Post these frames permanently in your classroom and reference them during every discussion. Consistency is key.

### 6-8: Content-Specific Frames

Middle school ELLs need frames tied to **subject-area language**. Science lab reports, math explanations, and literary analysis each require different academic vocabulary.

**Example frames for 6-8:**
- "The data suggests that _____ because _____."
- "The author uses _____ to convey _____."
- "To solve this problem, I first _____, then _____."

## 4 Classroom Activities Using Sentence Frames

### 1. Think-Pair-Share with Frames

Give students a sentence frame before the discussion. They complete it independently (*Think*), share with a partner (*Pair*), then present to the class (*Share*). The frame ensures every student has something to say.

### 2. Dialogue Strips

Print sentence frames as **cut-apart strips**. Partners take turns reading their frame and filling in the blank with their own ideas. This makes speaking practice feel like a game rather than a test.

### 3. Frame Journals

Students keep a journal where they use the weekly sentence frame to write about what they learned. Over time, they build a collection of academic sentences they can reference in future writing.

### 4. Frame of the Week

Introduce one new sentence frame each week. Post it prominently, model it Monday, practice it Tuesday through Thursday, and assess informally on Friday.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Too many frames at once** — introduce 1-2 at a time, not a wall of 20
- **Frames that are too simple for advanced students** — differentiate by proficiency level
- **Never removing the scaffold** — gradually reduce frame support as students gain confidence
- **Ignoring the bilingual connection** — providing the frame in both English and the home language accelerates understanding
