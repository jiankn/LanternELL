## What "Beginner" Means in ESL Vocabulary

Beginner = WIDA Level 1 (newcomer, 0-90 days in US schools) or Level 2 (some basic English). These students need vocabulary worksheets with very specific characteristics:

- **80%+ visual support** on every page
- **6-10 target words max** per worksheet
- **Picture-based directions** (no reading-dependent instructions)
- **Bilingual support** for Spanish-speaking students (most common newcomer language)
- **Tracing or matching tasks** rather than written response

Most "free ESL vocabulary worksheets" online are for intermediate-advanced ELLs and won't work for beginners. The list below filters out the misfits.

## Tips for Beginner-Specific Worksheet Selection

### Tip 1: Hold the Worksheet at Arm's Length
If you can tell what the topic is just from pictures, it works for beginners. If you need to read text, it doesn&apos;t.

### Tip 2: Count the Target Words
6-10 target words per page = beginner appropriate. 15+ = too many.

### Tip 3: Test the Directions
Cover the directions area. Can a 5-year-old figure out what to do? If yes, pass.

### Tip 4: Bilingual Test
If your student is Spanish-speaking, look for English-Spanish editions. Without them, retention drops 30-40%.

### Tip 5: Check for Mini-Books
Worksheets without a companion mini-book are missing the highest-impact instructional component.
