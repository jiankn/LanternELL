ESL writing prompts that work at one level often fail at another. Giving a Level 1 newcomer "Write a 5-paragraph essay about your hero" is asking the impossible. Giving a Level 5 student "Draw a picture of your family" wastes their capacity. This guide curates 30 ESL writing prompts organized by WIDA Level 1-5, with appropriate scaffolding for each.

For our complete library, see [ESL writing worksheets](/esl-writing-worksheets). For sentence frames, read [sentence frames for ESL writing](/teaching-tips/sentence-frames-esl-writing).

## WIDA Level 1 (Newcomer): 6 Prompts

Level 1 students are 0-90 days in US schools. Writing = drawing + 1-word labels.

1. **Draw your family. Label each person.** (mom, dad, brother, sister)
2. **Draw what you eat for lunch.** (pizza, apple, milk, bread)
3. **Draw your favorite color thing.** (1-word color label)
4. **Draw an animal you like.** (1-word animal name)
5. **Draw how you feel today.** (happy, sad, tired)
6. **Draw your bedroom. Label 5 things.**

**Scaffolding:** Picture model, native language acceptable, drawing primary.

## WIDA Level 2 (Beginning): 6 Prompts

Level 2 = some basic English. Writing = 1-3 word responses + sentence frames.

7. **My favorite food is ___.** (Picture frame for support)
8. **I have ___ in my family.** (List with picture support)
9. **I like ___ because ___.** (Two-word reason)
10. **My morning routine: First ___. Next ___. Then ___.**
11. **I see ___ at school.** (3 sentences using same frame)
12. **My family does ___ on weekends.**

**Scaffolding:** Sentence frames + picture support + bilingual translation.

## WIDA Level 3 (Developing): 6 Prompts

Level 3 = sentences with simple structures. Writing = full single sentences.

13. **Describe your bedroom in 5 sentences.**
14. **Write 3 sentences about your favorite holiday.**
15. **Compare two animals. What is similar? What is different?**
16. **Write a story about a time you helped someone. (5 sentences)**
17. **Tell about your weekend. What did you do?**
18. **Describe your school. (5 sentences)**

**Scaffolding:** Sentence starters, vocabulary list, picture prompt.

## WIDA Level 4 (Expanding): 6 Prompts

Level 4 = paragraph-level writing. Topic + supporting + closing.

19. **Write a paragraph about your dream job.** (5-7 sentences)
20. **What's the most important rule in your classroom? Why?**
21. **Describe a person you admire. What makes them special?**
22. **Compare your home country (or your family's culture) with your American school.**
23. **What would you change about your school?**
24. **Write a paragraph about your favorite book.**

**Scaffolding:** Paragraph template (topic + support + closing), word bank, peer revision.

## WIDA Level 5 (Bridging): 6 Prompts

Level 5 = multi-paragraph essays. Standard rubrics.

25. **Write a 3-paragraph essay about a person who changed history.**
26. **Argue: Should homework be banned? (3 paragraphs)**
27. **Write an opinion piece on social media's impact on teens.**
28. **Compare and contrast two different cultures.**
29. **Write a personal narrative about a time you faced a challenge.**
30. **Research and write about a famous scientist's contribution.**

**Scaffolding:** Standard rubric, optional outline template, peer + teacher review.

## How to Use Prompts in Your Classroom

### Differentiated Writing Block
- Same theme, different level prompts
- Each student gets the prompt at their WIDA level
- Same time allocation, different output expectations

### Daily Writing Journal
- One prompt per day from the appropriate level
- Build writing stamina across the week
- Friday: pick favorite to revise + share

### Writing Centers
- Multiple prompts in a folder at each WIDA level
- Students choose, complete during center rotation
- Self-check using rubric

## Common Mistakes with ESL Writing Prompts

### Mistake 1: Same Prompt for All Levels
"Write a paragraph about your family" overwhelms Level 1 and bores Level 5. Differentiate.

### Mistake 2: Too Open-Ended for Newcomers
Level 1 newcomers need closed-ended prompts (draw + label). Open-ended creates paralysis.

### Mistake 3: Prompts Without Scaffolds
Level 2-3 students need sentence frames. Level 4-5 need paragraph templates. Don't skip the scaffolding.

### Mistake 4: Cultural Assumptions
Some prompts assume Anglo-American background ("Write about your Thanksgiving"). Choose universal topics or include cultural alternatives.
