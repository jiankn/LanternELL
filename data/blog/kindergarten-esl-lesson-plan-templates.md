Lesson planning for kindergarten ELL students is harder than for general K students. You need to balance vocabulary instruction, phonics, social-emotional support, content integration, AND differentiate for varied proficiency levels — all without overwhelming a 5-year-old. This guide gives you 3 kindergarten ESL lesson plan templates that solve the planning problem: a first-week newcomer plan, a theme-week template, and a 30-minute mini-lesson template.

## Template 1: First-Week Newcomer Lesson Plan

When a brand-new K newcomer arrives, you have 5 days to establish: physical safety, emotional comfort, basic survival vocabulary, and connection to a buddy. Here&apos;s the day-by-day plan:

### Day 1: Arrival & Survival
- **Morning:** Introduce buddy. Tour classroom (point to bathroom, water, cubby, lunch area).
- **Vocabulary focus:** Name, hello, goodbye, bathroom, water (5 survival words)
- **Worksheet:** Bilingual name card + greetings frames
- **Assessment:** Can student point to bathroom + water on cue?

### Day 2: Classroom Routines
- **Morning meeting:** Repeat greetings. Add daily routine cards.
- **Vocabulary focus:** Pencil, book, chair, desk, paper (5 classroom objects)
- **Worksheet:** Classroom objects matching (8-card mini)
- **Assessment:** Can student point to each object?

### Day 3: Feelings & Connection
- **Morning meeting:** Add feelings check-in. Use bilingual feelings cards.
- **Vocabulary focus:** Happy, sad, tired, hungry, sleepy (5 feelings)
- **Worksheet:** Feelings faces matching
- **Assessment:** Can student point to "I feel ___"?

### Day 4: Numbers & Routines
- **Vocabulary focus:** Numbers 1-5 (essential for lunch count, lining up, etc.)
- **Worksheet:** Numbers 1-5 tracing + counting
- **Assessment:** Can student count 5 objects?

### Day 5: Synthesis
- **Whole-day review:** Re-circulate Day 1-4 vocabulary
- **Worksheet:** First mini-book combining colors + numbers + feelings
- **Assessment:** Can student read 3 of the mini-book pages independently?

By Friday, your newcomer has the absolute minimum vocabulary to navigate Week 2.

## Template 2: Theme-Week Lesson Plan

After Week 1 stabilizes, your K classroom shifts into themed instruction. Use this template for any K ESL theme (food, family, animals, weather, etc.).

### Monday: Introduction
- **Whole-group:** Show vocabulary cards. Pronounce each. Students repeat.
- **Read-aloud:** A picture book on the theme.
- **Worksheet:** Vocabulary card + matching worksheet (introduction)

### Tuesday: Practice
- **Morning meeting:** Quick chant of vocabulary
- **Center work:** Tracing worksheet + sorting activity
- **Whole-group:** Sentence frame "I see a ___."

### Wednesday: Application
- **Center work:** Fill-in-the-blank worksheet
- **Read-aloud:** Second theme book to deepen vocabulary in context
- **Whole-group:** Pair share — "Tell your partner one ___ you like."

### Thursday: Reading + Writing
- **Center work:** Mini-book reading + journal drawing
- **Whole-group:** Word wall update — add new vocabulary
- **Send-home:** Vocabulary cards for family practice (bilingual edition for Spanish speakers)

### Friday: Assessment + Celebration
- **Assessment:** Can student match 8 of 10 vocabulary words to pictures?
- **Celebration:** Theme-related song, game, or movement activity
- **Send-home:** Mini-book with bilingual sentence frames

This template fits within most K curriculum maps and gives ELL students 12-15 exposures per word across the week — the research-backed retention threshold.

## Template 3: 30-Minute Mini-Lesson Template

For pull-out ESL instruction or small-group differentiation, use this 30-minute template:

### Minutes 0-5: Warm-Up
- Greet each student (use their name)
- Quick chant of last week&apos;s vocabulary (review)

### Minutes 5-15: New Vocabulary Introduction
- Show 4-6 new vocabulary cards
- Say each word; students repeat 3 times
- Connect to a picture book or real object
- Use TPR (Total Physical Response): act out the word

### Minutes 15-25: Practice Worksheet
- Distribute one matching or tracing worksheet
- Walk around, support individual students
- Pair students with mixed proficiency

### Minutes 25-30: Closure + Send-Home
- Quick share: "Tell your partner one new word you learned"
- Send home: 4-6 vocabulary cards for parent practice
- Preview tomorrow

This template is designed for ESL pull-out (most common in US elementary schools), but works for whole-group K mini-lessons too.

## Adapting for Multiple Proficiency Levels

Every K classroom has multiple WIDA levels in the same room. Here&apos;s how to differentiate within the templates:

### WIDA Level 1 (Newcomer)
- Use only the first 4-6 vocabulary words from any list
- Always pair with a buddy
- Allow non-verbal responses (pointing, drawing, nodding)
- Bilingual editions when available

### WIDA Level 2 (Beginning)
- Use 6-10 vocabulary words
- Begin oral repetition expectations
- Sentence frame "I see a ___."
- Mix English and home-language responses

### WIDA Level 3+ (Developing)
- Standard 8-12 vocabulary words
- Expect 1-3 word oral responses
- Sentence frame writing
- Bilingual mini-book reading
