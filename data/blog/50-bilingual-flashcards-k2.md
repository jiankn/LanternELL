If you teach K-2 in an ELL or dual language classroom, you need bilingual flashcards. Not "vocabulary cards." Not "Spanish-only cards." **Bilingual flashcards** — English and Spanish on the same card with one shared picture. This list curates the 50 most useful ones, organized by theme, with notes on which to print first.

For a complete library and the research behind why bilingual flashcards work, see our main [bilingual flashcards](/bilingual-flashcards) page.

## Why 50 (and not 500)?

Vocabulary research is clear: K-2 students retain about 250-400 new words per year with strong instruction. Trying to flood them with 500 flashcards creates noise. The 50 below cover the highest-leverage Tier 1 vocabulary — the words students need every day in class, on the playground, at lunch, and at home.

## Tier 1: Daily Classroom (10 cards)

These are the **first 10 to print** for any K-2 newcomer or dual language student. Without these, the student can't navigate the classroom.

1. **pencil / lápiz** — every day, every subject
2. **book / libro** — reading, library, homework
3. **chair / silla** — seating instructions
4. **desk / escritorio** — workspace
5. **paper / papel** — writing tasks
6. **scissors / tijeras** — art, projects
7. **glue / pegamento** — crafts
8. **eraser / borrador** — corrections
9. **backpack / mochila** — arrival, dismissal
10. **lunchbox / lonchera** — cafeteria, lunch

## How to Use This List

### Week 1-2: Print all 50 as cards
Print on cardstock, laminate, and store in 5 labeled bags by theme. Hole-punch each set and put on a binder ring for quick access.

### Week 3-4: Whole-group introduction
Introduce each theme set in a 15-minute lesson. Show the card, say both words, students repeat. Add to the word wall.

### Week 5+: Centers and independent practice
Use the cards in 4-5 different center activities each week. For specific center setups, read [how to use bilingual flashcards in ELL centers](/teaching-tips/bilingual-flashcards-ell-centers).

### Throughout: Games and review
Don't drill. Use [bilingual flashcard games for beginners](/teaching-tips/bilingual-flashcard-games-beginners) to keep the energy high while reinforcing the same 50 words across the year.

## What About Tier 2 and Tier 3?

K-2 is the right time to focus exclusively on Tier 1 (daily-use, high-frequency vocabulary). Tier 2 (cross-curricular academic words) and Tier 3 (discipline-specific) belong in 3-5 and middle school. Trying to teach them in K-2 wastes instructional time and frustrates students.

If you have a strong K-2 student ready for more, expand within Tier 1 — add transportation, weather, places in the community. The themes are listed in our [bilingual flashcards](/bilingual-flashcards) library.
