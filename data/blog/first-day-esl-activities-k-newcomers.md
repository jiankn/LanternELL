A K newcomer's first day in your classroom shapes the next 6 months. Get it right and the student opens up. Get it wrong and you spend weeks recovering. This guide gives you an hour-by-hour first-day plan — exactly what to do, what to say, and what to avoid.

For 50+ activities throughout the year, see [50 hands-on ESL activities for kindergarten](/teaching-tips/50-hands-on-esl-activities-kindergarten). For the full activities cluster, browse [ESL activities for kindergarten](/esl-activities-kindergarten).

## Before They Arrive (Prep, 30 min)

Spend 30 minutes the morning of (or the day before) preparing:

- **Buddy assignment:** Pick a confident, kind student. Tell them they have a special job today.
- **Visual schedule on display:** Picture-based, posted at student eye level
- **Survival lanyard:** Pre-make a lanyard with picture cards for bathroom, water, help, sick (also bilingual if Spanish-speaking)
- **Classroom labels:** Make sure desks, cubbies, bathroom door are labeled with pictures + words
- **Bilingual welcome sign:** "Welcome / Bienvenido [name]" with the student's photo if you have it
- **Family communication form:** Bilingual basic info form for the parent

## 8:30 AM: Arrival (15 min)

### What to Do
- Greet at the door with a warm smile + simple "Hello, [name]"
- Walk them to their cubby (which has their name + photo)
- Show them their desk and chair
- Introduce the buddy: "This is [buddy name]. Friend."
- Sit with them while other students arrive

### What to Say (in their home language if you can)
- "Welcome." / "Bienvenido."
- "I am the teacher." / "Yo soy la maestra."
- "[Buddy name] is your friend." / "[Buddy name] es tu amigo/a."
- "I will help you." / "Te voy a ayudar."

### What to Avoid
- Don't bombard with English
- Don't ask "how was your trip?" or open-ended questions
- Don't stare at them or hover

## 8:45 AM: Morning Meeting (15 min)

### Activity: Bilingual Greetings + Class Introduction
- Sit in circle with class
- Use a soft toy as a "talking stick"
- Each student says: "Hello, my name is ___" and passes the toy
- Newcomer can pass without speaking, OR repeat "Hello" if comfortable
- Class repeats "Welcome [name]!" together
- Show class photo of newcomer's country on world map

### Goal
Newcomer feels seen, not interrogated.

## 9:00 AM: Tour the Room (15 min)

### Activity: Visual Schedule Walk
- Walk newcomer + buddy around the room
- Point to bathroom, water, snack basket, art supplies
- Use simple gestures + words: "Bathroom." Point. "Water." Point.
- Newcomer doesn't need to repeat — just observe

### Goal
Reduce uncertainty. Where is the bathroom? They now know.

## 9:15 AM: First Activity (Drawing) (20 min)

### Activity: Family Drawing
- Paper + crayons
- Buddy starts drawing their own family
- Newcomer can copy or draw their own
- No words required
- At the end, hold up newcomer's drawing: "Family. Mom. Dad."

### Why This Works
Drawing is non-verbal — newcomer can fully participate. Family is a universal topic. Buddy modeling reduces uncertainty.

## 9:35 AM: Snack Break (15 min)

### What to Do
- Sit newcomer next to buddy
- Don't force conversation
- Model: "I eat. I drink water."
- Let them watch and learn the routine

### Goal
Survival routine learned: where is snack, how do I eat with the class.

## 9:50 AM: Center Time (45 min)

### Activity: Buddy Visits 3 Centers
- Newcomer visits 3 centers with their buddy
- Buddy demos each center
- Newcomer participates non-verbally (sorting, drawing, building)
- Don't pull newcomer aside for "ESL instruction" today

### Why This Works
Newcomer learns through immersion + buddy modeling, not explicit instruction.

## 10:35 AM: Read-Aloud (15 min)

### Activity: Picture-Heavy Book
- Choose a book with simple, repetitive text + strong illustrations
- Examples: "Brown Bear, Brown Bear" / "The Very Hungry Caterpillar"
- Read in a sing-song voice
- Class chants the repeating phrases
- Newcomer can listen, observe, optionally chant

## 10:50 AM: Recess (20 min)

### What to Do
- Buddy takes newcomer outside
- Buddy shows playground equipment
- Other students gravitate naturally — let it happen
- Watch from a distance for safety

### Goal
Newcomer experiences pure play (universal language). This is often the highest-engagement moment of Day 1.

## 11:10 AM: Math (30 min)

### Activity: Counting + Sorting
- Math is mostly visual + manipulative — perfect for newcomers
- Counting bears, blocks, or buttons
- Newcomer counts in their home language; that's fine
- Sorting by color: also accessible

## 11:40 AM: Lunch (30 min)

### What to Do
- Buddy walks newcomer through cafeteria line
- Show: "Tray. Food. Water. Spoon."
- Sit them with class table
- Pre-arrange with buddy: "Help [name] today, please."

### Goal
Survive lunch. The hardest social-cognitive load of Day 1.

## 12:10 PM: Quiet Time (20 min)

### Activity: Looking at Picture Books
- Newcomer chooses any picture book
- No reading required, just looking
- Lower-stress recovery from lunch's social demands

## 12:30 PM: Afternoon (1.5 hours)

Afternoon is forgiving. Repeat morning patterns:
- Whole-group activity (more drawing, songs, simple games)
- Center rotation with buddy
- Read-aloud

Avoid introducing new high-cognitive-load tasks (no new math concepts, no spelling tests).

## 2:00 PM: Pack Up + Goodbye (15 min)

### What to Do
- Show: "Backpack." Help them pack their things.
- Repeat: "See you tomorrow!" / "¡Hasta mañana!"
- Hand them a small "Day 1 success" sticker or treat
- Make eye contact + warm smile at goodbye

### Goal
Newcomer leaves feeling: "I survived. I want to come back."

## What to Avoid on Day 1

- Don't give worksheets that require reading or writing
- Don't ask "Did you understand?" — they'll say yes either way
- Don't introduce phonics or letter sounds today
- Don't isolate them at a separate desk
- Don't translate everything aloud (creates dependency)

## Day 2 Preview

Day 2 builds on Day 1's foundation:
- Same morning routine + buddy
- Add 5-7 vocabulary cards
- First simple worksheet (matching only)
- Continue picture-heavy activities

By Friday, the newcomer should know: their cubby, their buddy, the bathroom, the water, the schedule, and 10-15 English words.
