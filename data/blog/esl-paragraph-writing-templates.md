Once ELL students reach WIDA Level 3, they're ready for paragraph-level writing. But paragraph writing isn't intuitive — students need explicit structures. This guide gives you 5 print-ready ESL paragraph writing templates covering the most common K-8 paragraph types.

For our complete library, see [ESL writing worksheets](/esl-writing-worksheets). For sentence-level scaffolds, read [sentence frames for ESL writing](/teaching-tips/sentence-frames-esl-writing).

## Why Paragraph Templates Matter

Paragraph writing is harder than sentence writing because it requires:

- **Topic sentence** that introduces the main idea
- **Supporting sentences** that elaborate
- **Closing sentence** that wraps up
- **Cohesion** between sentences

ELL students at Level 3-4 can write individual sentences but struggle to connect them into a unified paragraph. Templates externalize the structure until students internalize it.

## Template 1: Descriptive Paragraph

### Use For
Describing a person, place, thing, or event.

### Template

```
TOPIC SENTENCE: ___________________________________
(My ___ is ___.)

DETAIL 1: It is ___________________________________.
DETAIL 2: It also ___________________________________.
DETAIL 3: My favorite part is ___________________________________.

CLOSING: Overall, ___________________________________.
```

### Example
```
TOPIC: My bedroom is cozy and small.
DETAIL 1: It is painted blue.
DETAIL 2: It also has a soft bed.
DETAIL 3: My favorite part is the window.
CLOSING: Overall, my bedroom is the best place.
```

## Template 2: Narrative Paragraph

### Use For
Telling a story, retelling a personal experience.

### Template

```
TOPIC SENTENCE: ___________________________________
(One day, I ___.)

EVENT 1: First, ___________________________________.
EVENT 2: Next, ___________________________________.
EVENT 3: Then, ___________________________________.

CLOSING: In the end, ___________________________________.
```

### Example
```
TOPIC: One day, I went to the zoo with my family.
EVENT 1: First, we saw the lions.
EVENT 2: Next, we ate lunch.
EVENT 3: Then, we visited the monkeys.
CLOSING: In the end, it was a fun day.
```

## Template 3: Opinion Paragraph

### Use For
Sharing a viewpoint with reasons.

### Template

```
TOPIC SENTENCE: ___________________________________
(I think ___ because ___.)

REASON 1: One reason is ___________________________________.
REASON 2: Another reason is ___________________________________.
REASON 3: Most importantly, ___________________________________.

CLOSING: For these reasons, I believe ___________________________________.
```

### Example
```
TOPIC: I think dogs are the best pets because they are loyal.
REASON 1: One reason is that they protect their family.
REASON 2: Another reason is they help us exercise.
REASON 3: Most importantly, they make us happy.
CLOSING: For these reasons, I believe dogs are the best.
```

## Template 4: Compare-Contrast Paragraph

### Use For
Showing similarities and differences between two things.

### Template

```
TOPIC SENTENCE: ___________________________________
(___ and ___ are similar in some ways but different in others.)

SIMILARITY 1: Both ___ and ___ are ___________________________________.
DIFFERENCE 1: However, ___ is ___, while ___ is ___.
DIFFERENCE 2: Another difference is that ___________________________________.

CLOSING: Although they have differences, both ___________________________________.
```

### Example
```
TOPIC: Cats and dogs are similar in some ways but different in others.
SIMILARITY 1: Both cats and dogs are pets that live with families.
DIFFERENCE 1: However, dogs are loud, while cats are quiet.
DIFFERENCE 2: Another difference is that dogs need walks but cats don't.
CLOSING: Although they have differences, both make great pets.
```

## Template 5: Cause-Effect Paragraph

### Use For
Explaining why something happens.

### Template

```
TOPIC SENTENCE: ___________________________________
(___ causes ___ in many ways.)

CAUSE 1: First, ___________________________________.
EFFECT 1: This causes ___________________________________.

CAUSE 2: Next, ___________________________________.
EFFECT 2: As a result, ___________________________________.

CLOSING: Therefore, it is important to ___________________________________.
```

## How to Use These Templates

### Week 1-2: Modeling
Teacher fills in the template aloud. Students watch.

### Week 3-4: Guided Writing
Class fills in the template together. Each student copies into their journal.

### Week 5-6: Pairs
Partners fill in templates together with shared topics.

### Week 7+: Independent
Students fill in templates alone. Teacher conferences.

### Eventually: Fade the Template
By Level 5, students don't need printed templates. They've internalized the structure.
